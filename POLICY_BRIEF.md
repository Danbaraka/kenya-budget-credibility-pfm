# Policy Brief: Kenya Budget Execution Trends & MTEF Credibility (FY2022–FY2027)

**Author:** National Treasury Attachment / PFM Analytics Portfolio  
**Target Audience:** Public Finance Management (PFM) Practice, Genesis Analytics  
**Focus Areas:** Expenditure Credibility, Rigid Spending Dynamics, and Social Sector Space  

---

## 1. Executive Summary
Kenya's Medium-Term Expenditure Framework (MTEF) faces severe structural pressure from two converging forces: declining ordinary revenue productivity and rising non-discretionary commitments (Consolidated Fund Services: public debt redemption and pensions). While aggregate budget execution in Q1 FY2026/27 reached an apparent 95.3%, underlying performance reveals acute compositional fragility. Below-target performance in core ordinary tax streams—notably excise duties (82.8%)—was masked by ministerial Appropriations-in-Aid (A-i-A) outturns (112.1%). With rigid statutory claims projected to absorb 50.3% of ordinary revenue by FY2026/27, discretionary fiscal space for frontline human capital sectors (Health and Social Protection) has contracted sharply.

---

## 2. Key Diagnostic Findings

### A. The Structural Fiscal Space Squeeze
Between FY2013/14 and FY2026/27:
- **Revenue Trajectory:** Ordinary revenue as a percentage of GDP declined from **18.1%** to **14.3%**.
- **Rigid Spending Claim:** First-call nondiscretionary expenditures (debt service and pensions) increased from **18.0%** to **50.3%** of ordinary revenue.
- **Implication:** Over half of every tax shilling collected is committed prior to ministerial ceiling determination, directly constraining MTEF budget elasticity.

### B. In-Year Execution Asymmetries (Q1 Diagnosed)
- **Topline Distortion:** The aggregate 95.3% realization conceals significant variance:
  - *Excise Duty:* 82.8% of target.
  - *Non-Tax Revenue:* 74.8% of target.
  - *Ministerial A-i-A:* 112.1% of target.
- **Expenditure Drift:** Slide 2.1 data demonstrates expenditure exceeded in-year targets by KSh 116.9 billion, driving the Q1 deficit to KSh 329.1 billion against a target of KSh 174.8 billion.

### C. MTEF Sector Ceilings & Human Development Allocations (FY2027/28)
- **Capital & Basic Services Priority:** Education commands the largest ceiling share (**25.9%**, growing +18.3% by 2030), followed by Infrastructure (**19.7%**).
- **Social Sector Compression:** Frontline human development programs receive compressed baselines: Health (**6.0%**), Agriculture (**3.3%**), and Social Protection (**3.1%**). Agriculture exhibits the slowest medium-term growth rate (+4.1% through 2030).

---

## 3. PFM Strategic Recommendations
1. **Expenditure Commitment Controls:** Strengthen quarterly exchequer release alignments to actual exchequer cash inflows rather than linear quarterly allocations, mitigating emergency domestic borrowing spikes.
2. **De-bias Revenue Assumptions in BPS:** Calibrate MTEF revenue forecasts to trailing three-year empirical realization rates rather than nominal GDP elasticities to eliminate the structural deficit gap at source.
3. **Ring-Fence Basic Human Capital Subsidies:** Implement statutory spending floors for maternal/child health transfers and youth vocational programs to insulate social safety nets from in-year exchequer rationing.
